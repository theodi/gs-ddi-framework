Proficiency levels

Five levels, from Novice to Expert.

This is a maturity framework. Levels describe what strong performance looks like at each stage, not a minimum pass mark.

For most people, Working is a strong and appropriate target. A policy officer who uses data independently in their own cases, a delivery manager who applies digital tools reliably, or an operations colleague who follows and explains agreed standards is performing well. You do not need to be a Practitioner or Expert in every skill you use.

Higher levels are not a promotion ladder. Practitioner and Expert usually need either significant specialist expertise, or the authority and opportunity to lead, coach or change how work is done. Someone without that remit can still influence colleagues and improve team practice, but they should not be expected to demonstrate Expert on every strand.

What someone can show still depends on their role. Many civil servants shape how work is done without formal coaching titles. The right level is the one that matches the work in front of you.

Knowledge, skills and behaviours

Each skill is described in three ways. The tables below use these headings.

  • Knowledge: What you understand - concepts, principles and why they matter. Authoritative knowledge at Expert level signals specialist depth, not a requirement for every role.
  • Skills: What you can apply - practice in real tasks and situations.
  • Behaviours: How you act - habits, judgement and influence visible to others. Transversal-tagged behaviours are especially important for enabling leadership.

Level 1 Novice

Needs guided introduction to what the capability is, why it matters and how it contributes to public value.

The beginning of the journey - curiosity and willingness to learn matter here. Colleagues move on quickly where the capability is part of everyday work.

Typical roles: New starters; apprentices; colleagues new to digital, data, innovation or AI in their role; those returning after a long gap.

Knowledge
Has little or no practical knowledge yet. Can name the topic in broad terms and is beginning to see why it might matter.
Skills
Cannot apply the capability reliably without direct support. May try with close guidance and makes exploratory first attempts.
Behaviours
Shows curiosity and motivation to learn. Asks questions and is open to feedback without defensiveness.

Level 2 Awareness

Benefits from structured learning and practice with examples tied to real work.

A solid foundation - understanding why the capability matters and using basic elements reliably in familiar situations.

Typical roles: Operational delivery roles building foundation; administrators and caseworkers establishing reliable everyday practice.

Knowledge
Understands what the capability is and why it matters. Can recognise it in use and describe its importance in simple terms.
Skills
Uses basic elements with support. Identifies simple opportunities where it could improve clarity, efficiency or service quality.
Behaviours
Engages when encouraged. Tries new approaches with support and reflects on what worked.

Level 3 Working

Builds capability through ongoing practice and learning; may work toward accreditation where relevant.

Confident independent practice - the maturity target for most civil servants on strands they use every day. A meaningful mark of competence.

Typical roles: Policy officers, analysts, delivery staff and operations managers using the capability routinely; the benchmark for competent performance in most civil service roles.

Knowledge
Understands core concepts and methods. Has reliable knowledge and can explain benefits in the context of their own work.
Skills
Applies the capability independently in routine tasks. Adapts straightforward approaches to local needs with minimal guidance.
Behaviours
Demonstrates the capability consistently in everyday work. Takes responsibility for own development and follows agreed standards.

Level 4 Practitioner

Pursues advanced learning where relevant and helps others build confidence and consistency.

Practitioner is where the two routes become visible - you do not need both on every strand.

Typical roles: Where informal influence begins - experienced specialists others rely on, and team coordinators or workstream leads who improve practice around them.

Knowledge
Has detailed understanding including nuances and implications for quality and outcomes. Shares knowledge proactively with colleagues.
Skills
Uses the capability independently across varied scenarios. Troubleshoots complex issues and improves local practice.
Behaviours
Coaches and supports others informally. Models good practice and helps colleagues apply standards consistently.

Level 5 Expert

Undertakes specialist ongoing learning and uses expertise to elevate organisational capability and future practice.

Expert is one level with two valid demonstration patterns - you do not need both on every strand.

Typical roles: Heads of profession, lead specialists, programme and function directors, G6+ leaders with authority to set direction, and cross-government leads.

Knowledge
Holds authoritative expertise with insight into strategic value and system-level implications. Recognised as a trusted source of guidance in your domain.
Skills
Leads application across teams or services. Influences standards, governance or practice and drives improvement at scale.
Behaviours
Shapes culture and expectations. Champions responsible practice and develops capability in others systematically.

Recognising specialists and leaders

At Practitioner and Expert, the same level number can be reached in two valid ways.

Specialist depth

Authoritative knowledge and expert skills in domain strands - from the experienced colleague others rely on at Practitioner through to heads of profession at Expert.

Enabling leadership

Strong transversal practice - coaching, psychological safety, ethical judgement, collaboration and learning from failure. This route emerges clearly at Practitioner (team coordinators, workstream leads) and strengthens at Expert. Working-Practitioner knowledge is enough where your job is to commission, challenge well and support experts.

Mixed remit

Both patterns apply - for example a G6 profession lead who sets standards in their domain and shapes team culture.
