Data

Working responsibly with data - from everyday literacy and quality through analysis, evidence-based decisions and sharing. This domain is here because almost every role uses data somehow, even if you are not a data specialist foundational skills apply widely. Deeper quality, analysis and sharing expectations rise when data is central to your work or at higher grades.

Questions are grouped as knowledge, skills or behaviours and rated against five level descriptors. Both are described on the proficiency levels page. Some questions are also tagged with broader skills.

Clusters

  1. Foundational Data Skills
  2. Data Quality and Integrity
  3. Data-Driven Decision Making
  4. Responsible Data Sharing

Foundational Data Skills

Data literacy

Builds the foundational understanding, skills and behaviours needed to work confidently and responsibly with data, recognising that evidence-based decisions lead to better outcomes.

Knowledge

How would you rate your understanding of data and its role in decision-making?
1. Novice
Basic or minimal knowledge of what data is or how it supports decisions.
2. Awareness
Understands what data is, how it is created and stored, and why evidence-based decisions lead to better outcomes.
3. Working
Confidently understands core data concepts and how data flows through systems to support decisions.
4. Practitioner
Deep understanding of data as a tool to support decision-making.
5. Expert
Authoritative knowledge of data concepts and strategy; shapes data knowledge across teams.

Skills

How would you rate your competence in using data in your day-to-day work?
1. Novice
Finds and retrieves simple data with direct support, such as locating a record or looking up a figure.
2. Awareness
Uses basic data independently to complete simple tasks such as updating a record or checking totals.
3. Working
Uses data confidently and consistently in routine work; compares figures, checks accuracy and applies agreed ways of working.
4. Practitioner
Manages data across multiple tools; sets up shared spaces, models good practice and supports colleagues.
5. Expert
Leads how data is used across teams; designs consistent ways of working and drives improvements.
How would you rate your competence in using the data tools and systems relevant to your role?
1. Novice
Has little or no experience using data tools. Needs step-by-step instruction for the simplest tasks.
2. Awareness
Can follow instructions to complete basic tasks in familiar tools, such as opening a report or entering a value.
3. Working
Uses standard tools and systems independently to complete routine tasks such as updating records, running reports or checking totals.
4. Practitioner
Works confidently across multiple tools and systems, combining data sources and adapting to new tools with limited support.
5. Expert
Masters a wide range of tools and evaluates new ones. Advises others on the best tools for a given purpose.
How would you rate your competence in drawing on data rather than instinct or assumption when you need to make a decision?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking

1. Novice
Does not yet use data to inform decisions independently. Follows instructions to find or check figures.
2. Awareness
Uses simple data to help make basic decisions, such as checking a figure before sending a report.
3. Working
Applies data regularly to support decisions in own work; draws straightforward conclusions and shares findings clearly.
4. Practitioner
Uses data to make well-reasoned recommendations and challenge assumptions; helps teams make evidence-based decisions.
5. Expert
Shapes how data informs decisions at a strategic level; champions evidence-based thinking and builds capability across teams.

Behaviours

How would you rate your competence in making sure the data you work with and share is handled safely and responsibly?

Broader skills: Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Not yet aware of responsibilities around data handling. May make mistakes without realising the risk.
2. Awareness
Understands that data must be handled carefully and follows basic rules when reminded.
3. Working
Consistently follows data handling rules, protects sensitive information and raises concerns when something seems wrong.
4. Practitioner
Models careful and responsible data use, proactively identifies risks and ensures the team follows secure practices.
5. Expert
Champions responsible data use at a strategic level, leads on compliance and embeds a culture of data ethics.
How would you rate your competence in evaluating and improving the data processes you work with?

Broader skills: Continuous Learning, Systems Thinking

1. Novice
Does not yet evaluate data processes. Works within whatever is in place.
2. Awareness
Notices when something could work better and mentions it, even if not yet taking action.
3. Working
Takes steps to improve own data practices, applies lessons learned and shares what works with the team.
4. Practitioner
Identifies systemic issues and drives practical improvements, working with others to raise standards across the team.
5. Expert
Sets the strategic direction for data improvement, influences policy and governance and leads change that has lasting impact.
How would you rate your competence in questioning the data you use - for example, where it came from, how it was collected, or whether it tells the full story?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking

1. Novice
Accepts data at face value. Does not yet question sources, methods or representativeness.
2. Awareness
Begins to ask basic questions about data - where it came from, whether it looks right - when prompted.
3. Working
Routinely checks data for obvious issues such as missing values, outliers or inconsistencies before using it.
4. Practitioner
Critically evaluates data quality, bias and limitations; challenges poor data use and helps colleagues develop similar habits.
5. Expert
Shapes a culture of critical data evaluation across teams; sets standards for what counts as reliable evidence.

Interpreting visual data

Reads charts, graphs and other visual representations of data to extract meaning, identify patterns and support clear communication of evidence.

Knowledge

How would you rate your understanding of visual data - including how charts and tables work, and how visual formats can help or mislead?
1. Novice
Little knowledge of what visual data is or why it is used. May not yet know how to read a chart independently.
2. Awareness
Understands that charts and graphs present data visually to make patterns easier to spot and communicate.
3. Working
Understands a range of common chart types, when to use them and how to read them accurately.
4. Practitioner
Understands how design choices in visualisations can help or hinder accurate interpretation; knows when visuals can mislead.
5. Expert
Authoritative knowledge of data visualisation principles; shapes how data is presented to ensure clarity, accuracy and accessibility.

Skills

How would you rate your competence in reading and extracting meaning from charts and tables?
1. Novice
Can identify basic features of a chart such as a title or axis label but needs support to understand what the data shows.
2. Awareness
Reads simple charts and tables with guidance; identifies key figures and basic trends.
3. Working
Reads and interprets a range of charts and tables independently; spots patterns, checks accuracy and draws relevant conclusions.
4. Practitioner
Interprets complex or unfamiliar visualisations confidently; identifies limitations, questions assumptions and draws nuanced insights.
5. Expert
Analyses and evaluates visualisations critically; identifies when data is being misrepresented and advises on better approaches.
How would you rate your competence in creating clear and effective visualisations?

Broader skills: Collaborative Working

1. Novice
Does not yet create visualisations independently. Needs guidance to produce even simple charts.
2. Awareness
Creates basic charts using standard tools when shown what to do; follows templates or examples.
3. Working
Produces clear, appropriately labelled visualisations that communicate the main finding to the intended audience.
4. Practitioner
Designs visualisations that are accurate, accessible and tailored to the audience; advises colleagues on how to present data clearly.
5. Expert
Sets standards for how data is visualised across teams; ensures visualisations are clear, accurate and accessible for all audiences.

Behaviours

How would you rate your competence in considering whether the visual data you create or share works for people with different needs, including those using assistive technologies?

Broader skills: Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Not yet aware that visual data may be inaccessible to some people. Does not consider this when reading or creating charts.
2. Awareness
Understands that some people find certain visual formats difficult to use; begins to consider this when creating simple charts.
3. Working
Checks that visualisations are accessible - for example, not relying on colour alone - and considers whether the format works for all likely users.
4. Practitioner
Ensures accessibility and inclusion are built into how data is visualised across a team; reviews others' work and raises issues constructively.
5. Expert
Champions accessible and inclusive data visualisation; sets standards and ensures all visual outputs across teams meet accessibility requirements.

Managing data lifecycles

Handles data responsibly through each stage of its lifecycle - from collection and storage through to sharing, retention and disposal - following good practice to keep data secure, accurate and useful.

Knowledge

How would you rate your understanding of how data moves through its lifecycle from collection to disposal - including what happens at each stage and why responsible handling throughout matters?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking

1. Novice
Little knowledge of data lifecycle stages. May not be aware that data has a lifecycle at all.
2. Awareness
Understands that data has a lifecycle - it is collected, stored, used and eventually disposed of - and that each stage has responsibilities.
3. Working
Understands the key stages of the data lifecycle and the good practice principles that apply at each stage.
4. Practitioner
Understands how data lifecycle management affects data quality, security and compliance across a team or system.
5. Expert
Authoritative knowledge of data lifecycle management; shapes policy and practice to ensure data is handled responsibly at every stage.

Skills

How would you rate your competence in collecting and recording data correctly?
1. Novice
Collects or records data with direct support, following step-by-step instructions.
2. Awareness
Follows agreed procedures to collect and record data correctly, such as using the right format or completing required fields.
3. Working
Collects and records data consistently and accurately; follows standards and flags issues when data doesn't look right.
4. Practitioner
Reviews and improves data collection processes; ensures data is recorded consistently and to a high standard across a team.
5. Expert
Designs data collection processes that are accurate, efficient and compliant; sets standards across teams.
How would you rate your competence in storing and sharing data securely?

Broader skills: Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Needs support to store or share data securely. May not be aware of the risks of unsecured storage or sharing.
2. Awareness
Follows basic rules for storing and sharing data - such as using approved systems and not sharing sensitive data without permission.
3. Working
Stores and shares data securely and consistently, following agreed protocols and checking permissions before sharing.
4. Practitioner
Reviews how data is stored and shared across a team; identifies risks and puts improvements in place.
5. Expert
Sets standards for secure data storage and sharing across teams; ensures policies are practical, compliant and well understood.
How would you rate your competence in disposing of data correctly?

Broader skills: Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Not yet aware of data disposal requirements. Does not yet act on them independently.
2. Awareness
Understands that data must be disposed of correctly and follows instructions to delete or archive data when told to.
3. Working
Disposes of data correctly and on time, following retention schedules and disposal procedures.
4. Practitioner
Ensures data disposal is managed consistently across a team; reviews retention schedules and addresses backlogs.
5. Expert
Sets and oversees data disposal standards; ensures retention policies are legally compliant and consistently applied.

Behaviours

How would you rate your competence in making sure data is handled consistently and responsibly when it crosses team or organisational boundaries?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking, Collaborative Working

1. Novice
Handles data in isolation. Not yet aware of how own data handling connects to or affects other teams.
2. Awareness
Begins to understand that data handling decisions can affect other teams; raises questions when unsure how own actions fit into the wider picture.
3. Working
Considers how own data handling connects to other teams' work; flags issues that could affect shared data and follows up to resolve them.
4. Practitioner
Actively coordinates data handling across teams; ensures consistency, resolves cross-team issues and builds shared understanding of responsibilities.
5. Expert
Shapes how data handling is coordinated across the organisation; builds systems and relationships that ensure data flows reliably and responsibly between teams.

Data Quality and Integrity

Ensuring data quality

Reviews and improves data to ensure they are accurate, representative and fit for purpose, helping teams to make reliable decisions and maintain trust in information.

Knowledge

How would you rate your understanding of data quality and why it matters for decisions and outcomes?
1. Novice
Has no working knowledge of data quality. Begins to notice that some data seem more reliable than others and asks questions about why that matters.
2. Awareness
Understands that data quality affects how reliable decisions and services are. Knows that quality can be assessed across dimensions such as accuracy, completeness and consistency, and can explain in simple terms why poor quality data causes problems.
3. Working
Has sound knowledge of data quality principles and dimensions, and understands that quality is about fitness for purpose - whether data is good enough for the decision or task at hand. Familiar with government guidance on data quality and how it applies in their context.
4. Practitioner
Has detailed knowledge of data quality including how it interacts with the data lifecycle. Understands how quality issues compound across systems and teams, and knows that communicating quality transparently to the people using data is as important as improving it.
5. Expert
Has authoritative knowledge of data quality principles, dimensions and practices. Understands the importance of treating root causes rather than symptoms, anticipating how changes to systems or processes will affect quality, and driving a culture of continuous improvement at an organisational level.

Skills

How would you rate your competence in identifying, correcting and cleaning data to ensure it is accurate and complete?
1. Novice
Cannot yet identify or correct data errors independently. With support, begins to notice when something looks wrong, such as a missing entry or an obviously incorrect figure.
2. Awareness
Identifies common errors in simple datasets with guidance, such as duplicates, missing values or figures that don't add up. Makes straightforward corrections when shown how, and reports issues that are beyond their ability to fix independently.
3. Working
Applies routine checks to identify errors, inaccuracies and gaps in data within their area of work. Corrects and cleans data following agreed processes, documents what was changed and why, and escalates issues that require more specialist attention.
4. Practitioner
Reviews data systematically across a project or team, identifying patterns of error rather than isolated problems. Leads data cleaning activities, distinguishes between surface errors and deeper quality issues, and helps colleagues develop their ability to spot and correct problems.
5. Expert
Sets standards for how data errors are identified, corrected and documented across teams. Ensures checking and cleaning are built into processes rather than bolted on afterwards, and promotes a culture where quality problems are surfaced and treated at source.
How would you rate your competence in assessing whether data are representative and unbiased?

Broader skills: Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Cannot yet assess representativeness or bias independently. Begins to understand that data does not always reflect the full picture and asks questions about who or what might be missing.
2. Awareness
Understands that data can be incomplete or skewed in ways that affect decisions. With guidance, begins to ask basic questions about where data came from, who collected it and whether important groups might be missing or underrepresented.
3. Working
Applies straightforward checks to assess whether data are representative and free from obvious bias. Recognises that the choice of summary measure matters - for example, that a mean can mislead when data are skewed - and flags concerns about how data are being interpreted or used.
4. Practitioner
Systematically reviews data for representativeness and bias across projects, using evidence to challenge assumptions about what the data shows. Considers whether data collection and sampling methods are sufficient for the conclusions being drawn, and helps colleagues avoid common statistical pitfalls.
5. Expert
Sets standards for how representativeness and bias are assessed across teams. Ensures that data collection and management processes actively account for potential bias, and promotes a culture where the limitations of data are honestly acknowledged and communicated.

Handling data ethically and lawfully

Collects, uses and shares data fairly, securely and in line with laws and ethical principles, recognising that legal compliance is the minimum standard and that ethical obligations go further.

Knowledge

How would you rate your understanding of the legal and ethical framework governing how data should be collected, used and shared?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking, Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Has no working knowledge of data law or ethics. Begins to understand that there are rules governing how data can be used and that breaking them can harm people and organisations.
2. Awareness
Understands that data use is governed by law and ethical principles, and is aware of key legislation such as data protection law. Knows in simple terms what this means for everyday work - for example, that personal data must be kept secure and only shared appropriately.
3. Working
Has sound working knowledge of the legal and ethical framework governing data use, including data protection legislation and relevant government guidance. Understands that acting lawfully is the minimum requirement, not the standard to aim for, and knows when to seek advice from specialists such as data protection officers.
4. Practitioner
Has detailed knowledge of the legal and ethical framework, including how different pieces of legislation interact and how ethical principles apply in complex or ambiguous situations. Recognises that something can be legal but still ethically wrong, and can help colleagues navigate that distinction.
5. Expert
Has authoritative knowledge of the legal and ethical framework governing data use across government. Understands how legislation, policy and ethical standards interact at a strategic level, and actively promotes a culture where ethical data use goes beyond mere compliance.

Skills

How would you rate your competence in checking that data you access, use or share is handled lawfully and ethically?

Broader skills: Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Cannot yet make independent judgements about whether data can be accessed, used or shared appropriately. With support, follows clear instructions to handle data safely, such as using secure storage or not sharing files without permission.
2. Awareness
Follows established guidance when accessing, using and sharing data. Checks before sharing personal or sensitive data, uses approved channels and asks for help when unsure whether something is appropriate.
3. Working
Applies legal and ethical requirements confidently in everyday work when accessing, using and sharing data. Uses established tools and processes - such as data sharing agreements or impact assessments - to check that data use is appropriate, and raises concerns when something doesn't feel right.
4. Practitioner
Reviews how data is being accessed, used and shared across a project or team, applying appropriate tools and frameworks to assure both compliance and ethical standards. Identifies gaps or inconsistencies, helps colleagues navigate less straightforward situations and ensures practice goes beyond legal minimum.
5. Expert
Sets standards for lawful and ethical data access, use and sharing across teams. Ensures that established assurance tools and frameworks are used consistently, that legal compliance is treated as a baseline rather than a destination, and builds a culture where ethical considerations are part of every decision involving data.
How would you rate your competence in identifying and responding to legal and ethical risks or violations in how data is used?

Broader skills: Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Cannot yet identify data risks or violations independently. With support, begins to recognise situations where something might have gone wrong, such as data being shared with the wrong person, and knows to tell someone.
2. Awareness
Recognises common data risks and violations with guidance, such as unauthorised sharing or use of data for an unintended purpose. Knows who to report concerns to and follows clear steps when something goes wrong.
3. Working
Identifies legal and ethical risks in everyday data use and responds appropriately - escalating violations, following incident procedures and helping to limit any harm caused. Understands the difference between a legal breach and an ethical concern that falls short of breaking the law.
4. Practitioner
Reviews data use across a project or team to proactively identify legal and ethical risks before they become violations. Leads the response when problems occur, ensures lessons are learned and helps colleagues develop their ability to spot and respond to risks.
5. Expert
Sets standards for how legal and ethical risks are identified, assessed and responded to across teams. Ensures that risk identification is proactive rather than reactive, and promotes a culture where concerns are raised early and violations are treated as opportunities to improve rather than just incidents to manage.

Applying data standards

Uses agreed rules and formats to keep data consistent and interoperable, supporting effective data use, openness and collaboration across the Civil Service.

Knowledge

How would you rate your understanding of data standards and why consistency and interoperability matter across government?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking

1. Novice
Has no working knowledge of data standards. Begins to understand that data needs to be consistent to be useful and asks questions about why agreed rules and formats matter.
2. Awareness
Understands that data standards are agreed rules and formats that make data consistent and easier to share across teams and systems. Knows in simple terms why inconsistent data causes problems - for example, that different date formats or naming conventions make data difficult to combine or compare.
3. Working
Has sound working knowledge of data standards and why they matter for consistency and interoperability across government. Understands how applying standards in their own work contributes to data being usable beyond their immediate team or system.
4. Practitioner
Has detailed knowledge of data standards including how they interact with data sharing and open data requirements. Understands how standards enable systems to exchange data automatically and can make the case for consistent adoption to colleagues and stakeholders.
5. Expert
Has authoritative knowledge of data standards frameworks and their strategic importance for interoperability across government. Understands how schemas, APIs and related technical standards connect to wider data infrastructure and policy goals, and can guide teams through complex decisions about standards adoption and development.

Skills

How would you rate your competence in applying agreed data standards in your own work and flagging when data doesn't meet them?

Broader skills: Collaborative Working

1. Novice
Cannot yet apply data standards independently. With support, begins to follow simple rules for recording information, such as entering dates in a required format or using an approved list of categories.
2. Awareness
Follows agreed rules for recording and submitting information, such as using required formats, approved codes or consistent naming conventions. Recognises that doing this makes data easier to use and compare across teams, and asks for help when unsure which format or category to use.
3. Working
Applies agreed rules and formats consistently and independently when recording, submitting or sharing data. Checks own data before sharing, corrects simple inconsistencies and flags issues that require more specialist attention.
4. Practitioner
Applies data standards confidently across varied situations and checks that data meets agreed standards across a project or team. Spots and addresses inconsistencies, helps colleagues understand why standards matter and supports more consistent adoption. Contributes to decisions about how standards should be applied or updated in their context, including in relation to open data requirements.
5. Expert
Sets and maintains data standards across teams, ensuring consistent adoption and identifying where existing standards need updating or where new ones are needed. Provides guidance and training, engages with cross-government standards work including open data requirements, and ensures teams understand how their data connects to wider government data infrastructure.

Data-Driven Decision Making

Analysing data

Uses data and analytical tools and techniques to find patterns, draw insights and support evidence-based decisions that improve outcomes.

Knowledge

How would you rate your understanding of data analysis and how it supports decision-making - including what kinds of questions it can answer and what its limitations are?
1. Novice
Has minimal knowledge of data analysis but begins to understand that examining data carefully leads to better decisions. Recognises simple analytical outputs such as totals or averages with support.
2. Awareness
Understands that data analysis involves examining information to find patterns, differences or trends that support decisions. Knows that different analytical approaches suit different questions and that outputs need to be checked for accuracy.
3. Working
Has sound knowledge of core analytical concepts and methods. Understands how analysis connects to decision-making, including the importance of checking assumptions and being honest about what the data does and does not show.
4. Practitioner
Has detailed knowledge of analytical methods and tools, including when to use different approaches and when to involve specialist analytical colleagues. Understands the implications of analytical choices for the quality and reliability of conclusions.
5. Expert
Has authoritative knowledge of data analysis including advanced methods, the analytical landscape across government and the strategic value of robust analysis for policy and service delivery. Shapes how analytical capability is understood and built across teams.

Skills

How would you rate your competence in using analytical tools and software to work with data?
1. Novice
Uses simple tools such as spreadsheets with direct support to review small amounts of data; follows step-by-step guidance.
2. Awareness
Uses basic analytical tools reliably with light support; sorts, filters and compares data to identify simple patterns and differences.
3. Working
Uses analytical tools confidently and independently, including spreadsheet features such as pivot tables and charts; checks outputs for accuracy and knows when results look wrong.
4. Practitioner
Recognises when more advanced tools - such as data visualisation software or statistical packages - would better serve the analysis; can use or commission them and coaches colleagues on tool choice and use.
5. Expert
Understands the landscape of analytical tools including automated and machine learning approaches; guides decisions about when and how to use them and sets standards for tool use across teams.
How would you rate your competence in working out what question you need to answer and finding the right data to answer it?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking

1. Novice
Cannot yet identify independently what data would help answer a question. With support, begins to understand what is being asked and why.
2. Awareness
Identifies what data might help answer a simple question with guidance, such as what needs to be compared or measured. Asks for help when unsure whether the right data are available or suitable.
3. Working
Works out what question needs answering and identifies appropriate data to answer it. Checks that data are relevant, sufficient and suitable before beginning analysis.
4. Practitioner
Breaks complex or ambiguous questions into manageable parts and identifies the data needed at each stage. Helps colleagues sharpen their questions and challenges assumptions about what data will and won't show.
5. Expert
Shapes how questions are framed across teams, ensuring analysis starts from a clear question rather than available data. Sets standards for how data needs are scoped and documented.
How would you rate your competence in communicating analytical findings, including uncertainty and limitations?

Broader skills: Collaborative Working

1. Novice
Cannot yet communicate analytical findings independently. With support, begins to describe what data show in simple terms.
2. Awareness
Shares simple findings with guidance, such as summarising a key figure or trend. Begins to recognise that findings have limitations and flags uncertainty when prompted.
3. Working
Communicates findings clearly to colleagues, explaining what the data show and what they don't. Acknowledges limitations and uncertainty honestly rather than overstating what the analysis proves.
4. Practitioner
Presents findings to varied audiences, adapting language and format to suit the context. Clearly articulates confidence levels, caveats and the implications of analytical limitations for decisions.
5. Expert
Sets standards for how analytical findings are communicated across teams, ensuring uncertainty and limitations are consistently and honestly reported. Builds a culture where caveats strengthen rather than undermine analytical credibility.

Responsible Data Sharing

Collaborating with data

Works with other teams and organisations to share and reuse data safely and ethically, helping to create collaborative insight, efficiency and improvement.

Knowledge

How would you rate your understanding of data sharing across teams and organisations, including what enables it and what governs it?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking, Ethical Practice

1. Novice
Has no working knowledge of data sharing across teams. Begins to understand that data can be shared beyond their immediate team and asks questions about what makes that possible and what the rules are.
2. Awareness
Understands that data sharing across teams and organisations is governed by rules and agreements, and that it requires both technical and legal conditions to be met. Knows in simple terms why sharing data can improve outcomes and why those rules exist to protect people and organisations.
3. Working
Has sound working knowledge of what enables and governs data sharing across teams and organisations, including relevant legal frameworks, data sharing agreements and good practice guidance. Understands that both over-caution and careless sharing can cause harm, and knows when to seek advice.
4. Practitioner
Has detailed knowledge of the legal, technical and organisational conditions that enable effective data sharing. Understands how data sharing agreements, access controls and governance frameworks work in practice, and can identify and address barriers that prevent teams from sharing data effectively and responsibly.
5. Expert
Has authoritative knowledge of data sharing frameworks, standards and governance across government. Understands how legal, technical and cultural factors interact to enable or block data sharing, and can guide teams and organisations through complex or sensitive data sharing decisions.

Skills

How would you rate your competence in sharing data with other teams and organisations safely, lawfully and effectively?

Broader skills: Ethical Practice, Collaborative Working

1. Novice
Cannot yet share data with other teams independently. With support, follows clear instructions to share approved files or information using agreed channels.
2. Awareness
Shares data with other teams using approved channels and following established guidance. Begins to understand that how data is shared matters as well as whether it is shared - for example, that a spreadsheet is more useful to recipients than a screenshot. Asks for help when unsure about permissions or formats.
3. Working
Shares data with other teams and organisations safely and lawfully, following agreed processes and using appropriate tools and agreements. Chooses formats that recipients can actually work with rather than just formats that are convenient to send, and raises concerns when requests feel unclear or inappropriate.
4. Practitioner
Leads data sharing activities across projects, ensuring the right legal, technical and governance conditions are met. Helps colleagues navigate sharing requests confidently, addresses barriers that prevent legitimate sharing and ensures data is shared in formats that colleagues and partners can readily work with.
5. Expert
Sets standards for how data is shared across teams and organisations, ensuring processes are safe, lawful and as frictionless as possible. Identifies systemic barriers to sharing and works across organisational boundaries to remove them, promoting a culture where responsible sharing in useful, reusable formats is the default rather than the exception.
How would you rate your competence in finding, accessing and reusing data that others have shared?

Broader skills: Collaborative Working

1. Novice
Cannot yet find or access shared data independently. With support, begins to understand that data collected by others may already exist and could be useful for their work.
2. Awareness
Finds and accesses shared data using approved channels and following established guidance. Begins to think about what data might already exist before collecting new data, and asks colleagues who might hold relevant information.
3. Working
Finds, accesses and reuses data that others have shared, checking that use is lawful and appropriate. Proactively considers what data might help answer a question and who across their organisation might already hold it, rather than defaulting to collecting new data.
4. Practitioner
Identifies opportunities to reuse data across projects, thinking creatively about which teams or organisations might hold relevant data and brokering connections to make it accessible. Helps colleagues develop the habit of looking for existing data before collecting new data.
5. Expert
Promotes a culture where finding and reusing shared data is the default starting point. Works creatively across organisational boundaries to surface data that teams didn't know existed, reduces duplication across government and makes data more findable and accessible for others.

Behaviours

How would you rate your competence in working with others to make data sharing across your organisation easier and more consistent?

Broader skills: Systems Thinking, Collaborative Working

1. Novice
Begins to notice when data sharing between teams is slow or blocked and asks questions about why. Follows clear instructions to support simple data sharing tasks.
2. Awareness
Understands that effective data sharing depends on relationships and trust as much as rules and processes. Begins to build connections with colleagues in other teams and asks questions about what data they hold and how it might be shared.
3. Working
Actively builds working relationships that make data sharing easier and more effective. Approaches data sharing requests with an enabling mindset, looking for ways to share responsibly rather than reasons to refuse, and encourages colleagues to do the same.
4. Practitioner
Builds and maintains relationships across teams and organisations that enable regular, effective data sharing. Challenges a culture of unnecessary caution or restriction, helps colleagues develop confidence in sharing responsibly and models an enabling approach in their own practice.
5. Expert
Shapes the culture around data sharing across teams and organisations, making responsible openness the default rather than the exception. Builds networks and partnerships that unlock data sharing at scale, and champions the value of collaborative data use for improving outcomes and public value.
