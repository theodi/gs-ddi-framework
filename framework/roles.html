Roles and grades

How typical civil service grades relate to the proficiency levels.

This is a maturity framework. The bands below show where strong performance is most meaningful for a typical remit. They are not a pass mark or a promotion ladder.

Focus band and potential ceiling

The focus band is the range of levels that matter most for a grade. It is where you would usually expect to see and develop capability.

The potential ceiling is the highest level that is realistic for a typical remit at that grade. It is a potential, not a hard cap. Some people will go further where their role gives them the authority or specialist depth to do so. Many will not need to.

Grades

Administrative Assistant / Administrative Officer

Focus band: levels 2 to 3 (Awareness to Working).

Potential ceiling: level 3 (Working).

On foundational strands, Working is the maturity target for high performers - independent use of data, digital tools and systems in everyday casework. Awareness is the expected floor where the capability is in regular use. Practitioner behaviours (helping colleagues, modelling good practice) can appear informally even at this grade. Expert remains out of scope.

Executive Officer

Focus band: levels 2 to 3 (Awareness to Working).

Potential ceiling: level 3 (Working).

Working is the benchmark for competent performance where DDI & AI is part of everyday delivery. Briefing colleagues, checking quality and supporting new starters can evidence Practitioner behaviours on some strands. Expert authority is not expected.

Higher Executive Officer

Focus band: levels 3 to 4 (Working to Practitioner).

Potential ceiling: level 4 (Practitioner).

Working is the baseline; Practitioner is the aspiration where you coach colleagues, improve team practice or are relied on for advice in your area. Managing a workstream or small team offers scope to demonstrate influence beyond your own tasks. Expert outcomes need wider organisational remit.

Senior Executive Officer

Focus band: levels 3 to 4 (Working to Practitioner).

Potential ceiling: level 4 (Practitioner).

Practitioner is the maturity target for high performers - line management, commissioning work, quality-assuring output and shaping how your team works. Working remains the floor for independent delivery. Expert is aspirational where you hold cross-team or specialist authority.

Grade 7

Focus band: levels 3 to 4 (Working to Practitioner).

Potential ceiling: level 4 (Practitioner).

Practitioner is the benchmark - coaching, setting standards and improving practice in your team. Expert is an achievable aspiration where DDI & AI governance, profession leadership or system change is part of your accountability.

Grade 6

Focus band: levels 4 to 5 (Practitioner to Expert).

Potential ceiling: level 5 (Expert).

Practitioner is expected as a minimum for leading others' practice. Expert is the aspiration where you set standards, govern programmes or act as profession lead - shaping capability and direction beyond your immediate team.

Senior Civil Service

Focus band: levels 4 to 5 (Practitioner to Expert).

Potential ceiling: level 5 (Expert).

Practitioner to Expert is the maturity band - shaping culture, standards and organisational capability is core to the role. Expert descriptors reflect strategic authority where DDI & AI is part of your accountability.
