Broader skills

Five ways of working that appear across digital, data, innovation and AI. They are not a fifth domain.

How they are used

Related questions in the framework are marked as being indicators of application of broader skills. So as well as looking at capability in each domain, the framework also looks at cross cutting skills. These are often known as transversal skills.

The five skills

Systems Thinking

Analytical and critical thinking

Analyses how digital, data and AI systems and tools affect people, policies and institutions. Questions problems, interprets data critically, spots unintended impacts and chooses options that improve outcomes, not just add tech.

Digital Wellbeing

Supportive culture and psychological safety

Creates safe, inclusive spaces for colleagues and citizens to engage with digital change. Shows empathy, communicates clearly, addresss fears openly and models emotionally intelligent behaviours.

Ethical Practice

Trust, accountability and user focus

Makes responsible digital and AI decisions that protect rights and build public trust. Anticipates risks, designs around user needs and ensures fairness and safety while delivering value.

Collaborative Working

Teamwork and inclusivity

Brings together diverse perspectives to deliver joined-up digital services. Shares information, co-creates with users and coordinates work for benefits across the whole system.

Continuous Learning

Reflection & curiosity

Stays curious, experiments with new tools, reflects on outcomes, seeks feedback and refines practices to keep improving performance and service quality.
